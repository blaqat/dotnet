Frame

Why the current sheet reads as two fabrics

  1. Two grids that never agree. The knit repeats every 14×16 px and the diamond every 36×36 px. 36 isn't a multiple of 14 or 16, so every diamond edge cuts the V's at a different spot: it looks like a print laid over a knit, not one cloth.
  2. Three diagonals. The V legs lean about 62°, the diamond lines run at 45° and the cord frame twists at about 58°. Nothing lines up, so the eye reads noise.
  3. No hierarchy. The diamond and the knit have about the same faint contrast, so neither one reads as the ground.
  4. The dashes are used twice. The diamond is drawn with the same dashed line that means "sewn edge" on cards, chips and the sheet border, so the stitched edges stop standing out.
  5. Scale fights the text. A 36 px diamond is about 1.5 lines of body text, so it beats against the line rhythm.
  6. Cards blend in. Cards use the same knit at the same scale, just whiter and out of phase at their edges, so they look misprinted instead of sewn on.
  7. Contrast. At its darkest pixels the current sheet drops --ink-3 meta text to 4.0:1, and about 2% of the sheet is under AA. The diamond and the knit spend the contrast budget on each other instead of on one clear motif.
